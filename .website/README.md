The sky forgot its colors. Newton had a prism. You have a unicorn.

Your unicorn's horn shines white light. Drag and rotate prisms, raindrops, mirrors and filters to bend that light and bring color back to twelve levels of grey flowers. Every flower wants exactly one color, so white light alone won't do: you have to split it, steer it, filter it, and sometimes mix it back together.

Nothing here is a rainbow sprite. Every beam is traced live from Snell's law with wavelength-dependent refraction (Cauchy dispersion), so the spectrum fans out of a prism on its own, bends inside a raindrop, and bounces off mirrors without changing color. Each lit flower sustains the musical note of its wavelength, so a solved level plays a chord. The last level puts sunlight through a single raindrop and a real rainbow appears at 42°, red on the outside, exactly where the physics says it should.

How to play
- Bright pieces move; dim pieces are fixed.
- Drag a piece to move it. Drag its small ring (or use the mouse wheel, arrow keys or Q/E; Shift for fine steps) to rotate it. Drag the horn to aim.
- Release the mouse, or lift your finger, to lock a solution in.
- R resets, M mutes, Esc returns to the title. Progress is saved.

Twelve levels, five to eight minutes. Desktop and touch. Works in portrait, better in landscape.

Made in 13 KB with plain Canvas 2D and WebAudio, no engine. Sound effects via a trimmed ZzFX, compression by Roadroller. Source on GitHub.
